Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 15, 2026

1. Who we are

StreamSnatch is owned and operated by Gerald Buchanan. This policy explains how the StreamSnatch application ("StreamSnatch," "the app," "we," "us") collects, uses, stores, and shares information, including information received from Google APIs.

When these pages are hosted, the app home page is this site's homepage. Contact: streamsnatchergod@gmail.com.

2. What StreamSnatch does

StreamSnatch helps you clip live or recorded streams from Twitch, Kick, and YouTube into vertical 9:16 Shorts, with captions, hook titles, and an estimated virality score. If you connect a YouTube channel, the app can upload clips that you choose to that channel.

3. Information we collect

Depending on how you use the app, we may process:

  • Account and channel information you provide or that we read with your permission, such as a YouTube channel name, channel ID, and channel avatar.
  • OAuth tokens issued by Google so the app can upload clips you choose and read basic channel details.
  • Clip files, titles, captions, thumbnails, and related metadata that the app generates for you.
  • Streamer lists, settings, and usage logs needed to run the app (for example, whether a stream is live, and upload history).
  • Technical information such as local server logs.

4. Google user data (YouTube Data API)

If you connect a YouTube channel, StreamSnatch uses the YouTube Data API v3 and requests the following OAuth scopes only:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.upload — YouTube Data API scope used to upload videos that you choose to your own YouTube channel (including as YouTube Shorts) and to set the title, description, tags, and privacy or schedule status of those uploads.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/youtube.readonly — YouTube Data API scope used to read your channel name, channel ID, and avatar so the app can show the connected channel and confirm which channel will receive an upload.

StreamSnatch uses this Google user data only to:

  • Upload clips you explicitly choose to your own YouTube channel; and
  • Read and display your channel name and avatar (and channel ID) so you can manage connected accounts.

The app does not use Google user data for advertising, does not sell it, and does not transfer it to third parties except as needed to operate the YouTube API itself or as required by law.

5. How we store Google user data

OAuth access and refresh tokens, along with basic channel profile information (name, ID, and avatar URL), are stored on the app's server only so StreamSnatch can perform the uploads you request and keep your Accounts tab up to date. Tokens are not used for any other purpose.

6. How we share Google user data

We do not sell Google user data. We do not use Google user data for advertisements. We do not share Google user data with other people or companies for their own use. Humans do not read your Google user data except:

  • as required to operate or secure the app;
  • to investigate a security issue or abuse; or
  • as required to comply with law, a legal process, or a government request.

Uploading a clip you choose necessarily sends that video and its title, description, tags, and privacy settings to YouTube, which is operated by Google, so the video can appear on your channel.

7. Google API Services User Data Policy (Limited Use)

StreamSnatch's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

8. YouTube API Services

StreamSnatch uses YouTube API Services. By connecting a YouTube channel, you also agree to the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's own data practices are described in the Google Privacy Policy.

9. Other data the app processes

Stream content that you ask the app to clip may be downloaded temporarily in order to detect highlights and render Shorts. You should only clip material you have permission to use. Campaign or streamer research the app stores is used to help you decide what to clip, not for sale of personal data.

10. Retention

OAuth tokens and channel profile data are kept until you disconnect the channel in the app or revoke access in your Google Account. Clip files remain in the app's clip library until you delete them. Server logs are kept only as long as needed to operate and debug the app.

11. Your choices

You can disconnect any YouTube channel at any time from the Accounts tab in StreamSnatch. That removes the stored tokens for that channel from the app's server. You can also revoke StreamSnatch's access at any time at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions.

12. Children

StreamSnatch is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect Google user data from children.

13. Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the effective date above.
